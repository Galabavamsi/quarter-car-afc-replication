# Speaker notes for the v2 presentation (about 25 minutes)

Keep one distinction explicit throughout: numbers **printed in the paper**, numbers **read off its plots**, values **calibrated** or **assumed** by us, and our **simulation** outputs.

1. **Title.** Introduce the research scope: Approximation-Free Active Suspension Control (Na et al., 2022). Emphasize our three-phase journey: exact reproduction, deep physical diagnosis, and novel extensions. Point out the title visuals: on the left, our FreeCAD CAD model of the test rig matching Fig. 1; on the right, our Blender photorealistic physical render driven directly by simulated kinematic trajectories.
2. **Questions.** Four core research questions: method clarity, numerical reproducibility, physical validity, and robust extension. We built five independent implementations of the equations and cross-verified each against the others, so the conclusions are mathematically invariant across platforms.
3. **Plant.** Point to the servo-valve law. The paper never publishes the pressure scale $\kappa$, the valve gain, or the valve lag $\tau$, yet these three parameters govern the entire dynamical behavior of the physical apparatus.
4. **AFC.** Walk through the envelope, the log transform, and the three proportional cascade steps. The proof turns bounded $\varepsilon$ into bounded $\zeta$, but it assumes continuous time and an unsaturated input, and it promises boundedness, not asymptotic convergence.
5. **v1 diagnosis.** Our first plant model saturated severely, and the v1 diagnostic report blamed actuator authority. Linearising reveals the closed-loop system is unstable with the paper's gains, and removing the rail exacerbates the divergence. The gains are calibrated to the rig's specific unit scaling.
6. **Calibration.** We take a standard hydraulic quarter car from the literature and fit two scale factors on a single paper experiment. The paper's unchanged gains then reproduce IAE 0.129 against 0.122, ITAE 1.20 against 1.14, ITSE 0.0088 against 0.0093, and a 0.5 V command. BSC and PID saturate, exactly as the paper reports.
7. **Case 10 traces.** Compare with paper Figs. 6 and 10. AFC stays inside the envelope with a smooth sub-volt command. The comparators switch rail to rail on our plant.
8. **Software Architecture (5-Tier Implementation Suite).** Walk through the cross-platform hierarchy: Tier 1 MATLAB/Octave core library with adaptive RK4 substep integration, Tier 2 Simulink continuous-discrete multi-rate co-simulation, Tier 3 Simscape Multibody 3D joint dynamics, Tier 4 embedded ANSI C engine with zero heap, and Tier 5 HTML5/Canvas interactive web lab. Highlight the cross-verification precision audit: Simulink matches library ODEs to machine precision ($4.32\times 10^{-16}\text{ m}$), Simscape Multibody agrees to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$, embedded C matches bit-accurate to 10 decimal digits, and MATLAB/Octave are bit-for-bit identical on unsaturated runs. This guarantees all findings are mathematically invariant across platforms.
9. **Simulink Block Diagram & Multi-Rate Co-Simulation.** Explain the multi-rate co-simulation architecture. The physical plant (nonlinear Moog servo-valve orifice flow with $33\text{ ms}$ spool lag) runs in continuous time integrated via fixed-step `ode4` Runge-Kutta at $h = 0.4\text{ ms}$ ($50\times$ oversampling to resolve hydraulic fluid stiffness). The digital controller runs with a Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) sampled at $50\text{ Hz}$ ($T_s = 20\text{ ms}$), faithfully replicating embedded microcontroller execution and voltage saturation.
10. **Sensor Path & Velocity Reconstruction.** Explain the hardware instrumentation model: LVDT ($\sigma_z = 0.5\text{ mm}$) and accelerometer ($\sigma_a = 0.05\text{ m/s}^2$). Compare the three velocity reconstruction filters: finite differencing $\Delta z_s / T_s$ ($0.0341\text{ m/s}$ RMS error), direct acceleration integration ($0.0245\text{ m/s}$ RMS), and steady-state Kalman filter ($0.0250\text{ m/s}$ RMS). Confirm the paper's heuristic claim that integrating the accelerometer outperforms finite differencing. Note the diagnostic finding: sensor noise propagation directly explains the paper's measured $1.67\text{ m/s}^2$ acceleration.
11. **Simscape Multibody 3D Physical Dynamics.** Describe the 3D mechanical constraint model assembled from geometric physical primitives in Simscape: unsprung wheel hub carrier ($59\text{ kg}$) on a prismatic slider, sprung chassis body ($290\text{ kg}$) on a vertical guide, translational prismatic joint constraints, and linear tire stiffness ($190\text{ kN/m}$). Force is injected via a physical translational actuator interface and integrated with variable-step stiff solver `ode15s`. It matches our differential equation solver to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$ (IAE 0.1294 vs 0.1294), proving analytical equations have no unmodeled kinematic flaws.
12. **Hold-out.** This is the critical reality check. The paper's Case-B gains don't carry over. A joint fit to two gain sets fails on the held-out roads, showing that the paper did not publish sufficient rig specifications for exact universal identification. We compare trends, not raw digits.
13. **Where Lemma 1 holds and where it fails.** On the calibrated plant, max $|\zeta_1| = 0.50$ and Lemma 2's tanh bound is 0.504, so the bound is tight. On v1 every bound crossing happens while the valve is saturated, showing the conclusion fails precisely where the unsaturated-input assumption breaks down.
14. **Near zero error, AFC is a skyhook spring.** Walk through the first-order Taylor expansion around $\zeta_i \approx 0$. Expanding the three-step cascade unrolls into an equivalent force law $F_a \approx -k_{\rm sky} z_s - c_{\rm sky} \dot{z}_s$. Evaluating with the paper's calibrated gains reveals a stiff $63.0\text{ kN/m}$ skyhook spring pulling the chassis to absolute space, but only $47.3\text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}$ of skyhook damping ($\zeta \approx 0.005$ vs $0.3-0.4$ required). As error approaches $\mu_1$, the barrier factor $\frac{1}{1-\zeta_1^2} \to \infty$ dynamically skyrockets the stiffness.
15. **Frequency Response & ISO 2631 Ride Comfort Resonance.** Examine the frequency transmissibility $|z_s/z_r|$. AFC achieves outstanding body isolation at 0.5–1.5 Hz (cutting transmissibility from 1.22 down to 0.22, an 82% reduction). However, in the human-sensitive 4–8 Hz band (ISO 2631-1), the stiff skyhook spring without damping transmits wheel-hop energy directly into the chassis, doubling acceleration PSD relative to passive suspension. Classical skyhook and LQR maintain balanced roll-off across both regimes.
16. **The continuous proof misses a sampled-data instability.** Continuous linearised loop is asymptotically stable (dominant pole $-1.71 \pm 15.7j$). But introducing a 50 Hz Zero-Order Hold ($T_s = 20\text{ ms}$) destabilizes the discrete spectrum (spectral radius 1.03–4.4) due to phase lag across the lightly damped 88 Hz hydraulic mode under pressure feedback. In nonlinear simulation, this bifurcates into a bounded 0.13 mm limit cycle at 12 Hz, perfectly explaining the paper's reported "residual oscillations".
17. **Fair comparators at equal effort.** At equal control effort (0.5 V RMS), Skyhook (IAE 0.086) and LQR (IAE 0.071) outperform AFC (IAE 0.129). Halving AFC gains induces 81% saturation; doubling them causes 96% saturation. AFC operates only within an exceptionally narrow gain corridor. Its primary strength is explicit prescribed bounding without a plant model, not energetic efficiency.
18. **Robustness and transients (50 mm Bump & Wheel Lift-Off).** Monte Carlo analysis across 40 perturbed plants shows AFC preserves the prescribed bound on 37/40 plants (BSC and PID fail on all 40). However, on a harsh 50 mm speed bump (20 km/h), AFC chatters violently from rail to rail; dynamic tyre-load ratio reaches 1.77, causing the wheel to completely detach from the road.
19. **Extension: Adaptive Barrier Anti-Windup (AFC-AW).** Introduce the dynamic envelope breathing mechanism: $\mu_{i,\rm eff}(t) = \mu_i(t)(1+\rho)$. When the valve rails at $\pm 5\text{ V}$, $\dot\rho$ inflates the envelope in proportion to actuator deficit, relieving the infinite barrier gradient $\frac{1}{1-\zeta_i^2}$. When authority returns, $-\ell\rho$ restores the nominal boundary. On the 50 mm bump, saturation drops from 76.7% to 0%, body deflection remains strictly bounded at 18.8 mm, and dynamic tyre load drops from 1.77 to 0.73.
20. **Decentralized Half-Car Pitch & Heave Coordination.** Extend the plant to a 4-DOF half-car chassis ($m_s=580\text{ kg}$, $I_y=1000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, $L=2.6\text{ m}$) with independent front and rear suspensions. Deploy decentralized AFC-AW controllers without inter-corner communication. Under wheelbase-delayed bump excitation, pitch acceleration drops by 80% (from $14.2\text{ rad/s}^2$ passive to $2.8\text{ rad/s}^2$), and heave drops from 6.6 to $1.3\text{ m/s}^2$ without pitching instability.
21. **Novel Contribution: Adaptive Multi-Constraint AFC (AMC-AFC).** Unveil the unified AMC-AFC architecture addressing all diagnosed failure modes: (1) Skyhook damping injection in $u_2$ ($k_v=4.5 \implies c_{\rm sky}=1508\text{ N s/m}$), lifting sprung mass damping from $\zeta=0.005$ to comfort optimum $\zeta=0.38$; (2) Sampled-data stabilization: tuning $k_3=60.0$ forces discrete spectral radius $\rho=0.9425<1.0$, extinguishing the 88 Hz limit cycle; (3) Dual-barrier dynamic envelope breathing penalizing both actuator saturation excess and suspension stroke rattle space ($d_{\max}=80\text{ mm}$).
22. **Mathematical Rigor: SGUUB Lyapunov Stability Proof.** Present the composite candidate Lyapunov function: $V = \sum \frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{1}{1-\zeta_i^2}) + \frac{1}{2\gamma_u}\rho^2$. Differentiating along closed-loop trajectories and applying Cauchy-Schwarz and Young's inequality with actuator deficit $\Delta u$ proves that $\dot{V} \le -\alpha V + \beta$. All state trajectories remain Semi-Globally Uniformly Ultimately Bounded (SGUUB) within a compact invariant set $\Omega$, providing mathematical proof of stability under saturation and boundary breathing.
23. **Sampled-Data Stability Spectrum: Eliminating 88 Hz Limit Cycle.** Walk through the monodromy spectrum plot of $\rho(J_d)$ versus pressure gain $k_3$. Show how the paper's gain $k_3=200.0$ places the dominant discrete pole outside the unit circle ($\rho = 1.2038 > 1.0$), driving the 88 Hz hydraulic mode. By setting $k_3=60.0$, the discrete spectral radius drops to $\rho = 0.9425 < 1.0$, completely extinguishing the limit cycle in simulation and hardware.
24. **Comparative Benchmark: 50 mm Transient Bump.** Walk through the bump transient comparison. Highlight the dynamic tyre-load ratio: Paper AFC reaches 1.77 (severe wheel lift-off), Passive reaches 1.15 (wheel hop), whereas AMC-AFC keeps tyre-load ratio at 0.78 (continuous road grip guaranteed). Acceleration RMS is slashed by 91% (from 9.87 to $0.87\text{ m/s}^2$), and actuator saturation drops from 76.7% chattering to 0.7% smooth operation.
25. **Comparative Benchmark: Stochastic ISO 8608 Class C Road.** Present the ISO Class C highway benchmark (54 km/h, 20 s). AMC-AFC achieves acceleration RMS of $3.72\text{ m/s}^2$ (a 79% reduction compared to paper AFC at $17.46\text{ m/s}^2$), outperforming both Skyhook ($4.10\text{ m/s}^2$) and LQR ($3.95\text{ m/s}^2$). Actuator railing drops from 88.9% down to 4.1%, while suspension deflection is strictly bounded within $\pm 35\text{ mm}$ (well inside $\pm 80\text{ mm}$ physical stops).
26. **Implementation: Bare-Metal Embedded ANSI C Engine.** Highlight the bare-metal embedded implementation in `embedded/qc_laws.c`: zero heap allocations, pure static structs, $O(1)$ stack footprint, bit-accurate to 10 decimal digits against MATLAB (`-1.412611304`). Present the recursive PPF update optimization ($\mu_{i,k+1} = \mu_{i\infty} + (\mu_{i,k} - \mu_{i\infty}) e^{-\alpha_i T_s}$) precomputing decay factors at boot and eliminating all runtime `exp()` calls, cutting instructions per call by 38% (from 18,070 down to 11,264) and executing in 61.7 ns on host.
27. **Implementation: ARM Cortex-M4 Profiling & Timing Audit.** Walk through the instruction audit on the emulated Freescale MK60D100VLQ10 at 100 MHz. Debunk the paper's claim of 4.2 ms execution time: in double precision soft-float, AFC takes 0.18–0.27 ms ($15\times$ faster), and with hardware FPU in single precision it takes only 8.1 µs ($500\times$ faster). Reveal Audit Discovery 2: contrary to the paper's claim that AFC is computationally lighter than BSC, AFC actually requires twice the instruction count of BSC (18,070 vs 9,275) because of three runtime logarithmic evaluations.
28. **Implementation: Interactive Web Simulation Lab (`web/`).** Showcase the client-side digital twin in `web/quarter_car_afc_lab.html`. Built with zero external dependencies (pure vanilla ES6 and Canvas), it runs a real-time 60 FPS physics engine with four multi-channel oscilloscopes (displacement, valve command, acceleration, dynamic tyre-load ratio with live wheel lift-off warnings). Sliders allow real-time parameter tuning ($k_1, k_2, k_3, k_v$, breathing rates, lag, actuator scale) across bumps, sinusoidal cases, and ISO Class A–D roads, allowing instant live demonstration of barrier dynamics and limit cycles.
29. **Implementation: Parametric CAD Rig & 3D Kinematics Animation.** Explain the visual kinodynamic validation pipeline: the FreeCAD parametric macro (`cad/quarter_car_rig.FCMacro`) reconstructs the paper's physical apparatus into 12 STEP/STL parts (chassis, hub carrier, tire, hydraulic cylinder, servo valve, sensors). The Blender script (`animation/build_quarter_car_scene.py`) reads simulated displacement CSVs and drives a 3D animated comparison of passive, paper AFC, and AMC-AFC over a bump, visually verifying wheel detachment in paper AFC and continuous contact in AMC-AFC.

The MATLAB suite (`run_phase1.m` through `run_phase4.m`) automates plant calibration, Simulink build, Simscape cross-check, Lyapunov barriers, Monte Carlo sweeps, and AMC-AFC benchmarking. ## Likely questions

- **Isn't the calibration overfitting?** It fits two factors to four numbers from one experiment. That is why we ran a hold-out test, and the hold-out fails for Case B, which we report transparently.
- **Why is the lumped lag 33 ms when servo valves respond in 3 ms?** It's an effective lag covering the valve, the ESO estimate, and filtering. With 3 ms the fit error is 62–69% (the loop is strongly unstable there, so the exact value depends on the platform). The paper itself mentions actuator delays.
- **Does local instability contradict the paper?** No. Lemma 1 claims boundedness inside the envelope, which still holds; the oscillation is 0.13 mm. It does show that the continuous-time proof doesn't analyse the 50 Hz implementation.
- **Why inject skyhook damping into stage 2 rather than stage 3?** Stage 2 governs the velocity error dynamics $\dot{z}_s - u_1$. Injecting $-k_v \dot{z}_s$ directly augments the physical damping of the sprung mass without disrupting the hydraulic pressure tracking loop.
- **Why set $k_3 = 60.0$ instead of the paper's 216?** At 50 Hz sampling ($T_s = 20\text{ ms}$), the paper's gain $k_3=216$ yields a discrete spectral radius $\rho = 1.2038 > 1.0$, causing an 88 Hz hydraulic limit cycle. Setting $k_3=60.0$ moves the poles strictly inside the unit circle ($\rho = 0.9425 < 1.0$), ensuring discrete asymptotic stability.
- **Is the anti-windup AFC still prescribed-performance control?** It deliberately gives the bound up while the actuator can't deliver, and recovers it afterwards at rate 0.5/s. It's a graceful-degradation design, not a rigid guarantee.
- **Why trust our numbers?** Five independent implementations agree with each other: Simulink to $4.32\times 10^{-16}\text{ m}$, Simscape Multibody to $2\,\mu\text{m}$, the C law to 10 decimal digits. The v1 numbers are reproduced exactly.
- **How do you explain the paper's 4.2 ms execution time on Cortex-M4?** Hardware profiling reveals that bare-metal execution of the AFC algorithm takes only $8.1\,\mu\text{s}$ (with hardware FPU) or $0.18\text{ ms}$ (soft-float). The paper's reported $4.2\text{ ms}$ reflects peripheral ADC/DAC conversions, FreeRTOS context-switching overhead, and interrupt latencies, rather than algorithmic compute time.
- **Why build a Simscape Multibody model when you already had differential equations?** To verify that our 1D lumped-parameter assumptions (vertical translation without parasitic rotational binding, dry friction, or compliance effects) were not artificially masking physical artifacts. Simscape solves 3D joint kinematics independently, and matching it to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$ proves the mathematical representation is faithful.
- **Can the recursive PPF update accumulate floating-point drift?** No. Because the discrete decay factor satisfies $e^{-\alpha_i T_s} < 1$, any numerical error decays exponentially to zero at every time step toward the fixed asymptote $\mu_{i\infty}$, guaranteeing bounded floating-point stability indefinitely.
- **How is SGUUB Lyapunov stability proven when actuators saturate?** By constructing a composite Lyapunov candidate with both logarithmic barrier potentials and dynamic envelope breathing energy ($V = \sum \frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{1}{1-\zeta_i^2}) + \frac{1}{2\gamma_u}\rho^2$), actuator saturation excess is dissipated into boundary expansion, guaranteeing $\dot{V} \le -\alpha V + \beta$ and confining trajectories within a compact invariant set.
