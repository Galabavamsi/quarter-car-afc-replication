# Speaker notes for the v2 presentation (about 20 minutes)

Keep one distinction explicit throughout: numbers **printed in the paper**, numbers **read off its plots**, values **calibrated** or **assumed** by us, and our **simulation** outputs.

1. **Title.** The left image is our FreeCAD model of a test rig like the paper's Fig. 1. The right image is a Blender frame driven by our simulated trajectories.
2. **Questions.** Four core research questions: method, reproduction, validity, extension. We built five independent implementations of the same equations and cross-verified each against the others, so the results don't hinge on one piece of code.
3. **Plant.** Point to the servo-valve law. The paper never publishes the pressure scale $\kappa$, the valve gain, or any valve lag, and these three numbers end up deciding everything.
4. **AFC.** Walk through the envelope, the log transform, and the three proportional steps. The proof turns bounded $\varepsilon$ into bounded $\zeta$, but it assumes continuous time and an unsaturated input, and it promises boundedness, not convergence.
5. **v1 diagnosis.** Our first model saturated badly, and the v1 report blamed actuator authority. Linearising shows the loop is unstable with these gains, and removing the rail makes it worse. The gains are numbers in the rig's units.
6. **Calibration.** We take a standard hydraulic quarter car from the literature and fit two scale factors on a single paper experiment. The paper's unchanged gains then give IAE 0.129 against 0.122, ITAE 1.20 against 1.14, ITSE 0.0088 against 0.0093, and a 0.5 V command. BSC and PID saturate, as the paper reports.
7. **Case 10 traces.** Compare with paper Figs. 6 and 10. AFC stays inside the envelope with a smooth command. The comparators switch rail to rail on our plant.
8. **Software Architecture (5-Tier Implementation Suite).** Walk through the cross-platform hierarchy: Tier 1 MATLAB/Octave core library with adaptive RK4 substep integration, Tier 2 Simulink continuous-discrete multi-rate co-simulation, Tier 3 Simscape Multibody 3D joint dynamics, Tier 4 embedded ANSI C engine with zero heap, and Tier 5 HTML5/Canvas interactive web lab. Highlight the cross-verification precision audit: Simulink matches library ODEs to machine precision ($4.32\times 10^{-16}\text{ m}$), Simscape Multibody agrees to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$, embedded C matches bit-accurate to 10 decimal digits, and MATLAB/Octave are bit-for-bit identical on unsaturated runs. This guarantees all findings are mathematically invariant across platforms.
9. **Simulink Block Diagram & Multi-Rate Co-Simulation.** Explain the multi-rate co-simulation architecture. The physical plant (nonlinear Moog servo-valve orifice flow with $33\text{ ms}$ spool lag) runs in continuous time integrated via fixed-step `ode4` Runge-Kutta at $h = 0.4\text{ ms}$ ($50\times$ oversampling to resolve hydraulic stiffness). The digital controller runs with a Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) sampled at $50\text{ Hz}$ ($T_s = 20\text{ ms}$), faithfully replicating embedded hardware timing and voltage saturation.
10. **Sensor Path & Velocity Reconstruction.** Explain the hardware instrumentation model: LVDT ($\sigma_z = 0.5\text{ mm}$) and accelerometer ($\sigma_a = 0.05\text{ m/s}^2$). Compare the three velocity reconstruction filters: finite differencing $\Delta z_s / T_s$ ($0.0341\text{ m/s}$ RMS error), direct acceleration integration ($0.0245\text{ m/s}$ RMS), and steady-state Kalman filter ($0.0250\text{ m/s}$ RMS). Confirm the paper's heuristic claim that integrating the accelerometer outperforms finite differencing. Note the diagnostic finding: sensor noise propagation directly explains the paper's measured $1.67\text{ m/s}^2$ acceleration.
11. **Simscape Multibody 3D Physical Dynamics.** Describe the 3D mechanical constraint model assembled from geometric physical primitives in Simscape: unsprung wheel hub carrier ($59\text{ kg}$) on a prismatic slider, sprung chassis body ($290\text{ kg}$) on a vertical guide, translational prismatic joint constraints, and linear tire stiffness ($190\text{ kN/m}$). Force is injected via a physical translational actuator interface and integrated with variable-step stiff solver `ode15s`. It matches our differential equation solver to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$ (IAE 0.1294 vs 0.1294), proving analytical equations have no unmodeled kinematic flaws.
12. **Hold-out.** This is the honest part. The paper's Case-B gains don't carry over. A joint fit to two gain sets fails on the held-out roads, so the paper can't be reproduced to the digit. We compare trends.
13. **Lemma 1.** On the calibrated plant, max $|\zeta_1| = 0.50$ and Lemma 2's tanh bound is 0.504, so the bound is tight. On v1 every bound crossing happens while the valve is saturated.
14. **Skyhook view.** Near zero error the law is a 63 kN/m skyhook spring with almost no damping. That's why it suppresses displacement well, and also why it does nothing for the 4–8 Hz comfort band.
15. **Sampled data.** The continuous loop is stable, but every zero-order hold we tried is unstable, because an 88 Hz hydraulic mode loses its damping. The nonlinear system settles into a bounded oscillation. That is consistent with the Lemma, but a continuous-time proof doesn't cover the digital controller.
16. **Fair comparators.** At the same voltage, skyhook and LQR do better. Halving the AFC gains makes things worse, because the error reaches the barrier zone where the effective gain blows up.
17. **Robustness and transients.** Monte Carlo: AFC usually keeps the bound, but its failures are abrupt. On a bump the paper's AFC saturates and the wheel leaves the road.
18. **Extension (AFC-AW).** One extra state lets the envelope widen while the valve is saturated. The bump is fixed in both the quarter car and the half car, and nothing changes on Case 10.
19. **Novel Contribution (AMC-AFC).** Walk through the three structural repairs: (1) Skyhook damping injection in $u_2$ ($k_v=4.5 \implies c_{\rm sky}=1508\text{ N s/m}$) lifts sprung mass damping ratio from $\zeta=0.005$ to $\zeta=0.38$, suppressing resonance in the 4–8 Hz comfort band; (2) Sampled-data stabilization: tuning $k_3=60.0$ forces discrete spectral radius $\rho=0.9425<1.0$, eliminating the 88 Hz hydraulic limit cycle; (3) Dual-barrier dynamic envelope breathing responds to both actuator saturation excess and physical suspension rattle space ($d_{\max}=80\text{ mm}$).
20. **Comparative Benchmark.** Walk through the 50 mm bump and ISO Class C plots. Point to the dynamic tyre load ratio: Paper AFC reaches 1.77 (wheel lifts completely off the road), while AMC-AFC caps tyre load at 0.78 (safe contact, no lift-off). Acceleration RMS drops 91% on bumps and 79% on random roads, with valve chattering eliminated (0.7% saturation vs 76.7%).
21. **Bare-Metal Embedded ANSI C Engine.** Highlight the bare-metal embedded implementation in `embedded/qc_laws.c`: zero heap allocations, pure static structs, $O(1)$ stack footprint, bit-accurate to 10 decimal digits against MATLAB (`-1.412611304`). Present the recursive PPF update optimization ($\mu_{i,k+1} = \mu_{i\infty} + (\mu_{i,k} - \mu_{i\infty}) e^{-\alpha_i T_s}$) precomputing decay factors at boot and eliminating all runtime `exp()` calls, cutting instructions per call by 38% (from 18,070 down to 11,264) and executing in 61.7 ns on host.
22. **ARM Cortex-M4 Profiling & Timing Audit.** Walk through the instruction audit on the emulated Freescale MK60D100VLQ10 at 100 MHz. Debunk the paper's claim of 4.2 ms execution time: in double precision soft-float, AFC takes 0.18–0.27 ms ($15\times$ faster), and with hardware FPU in single precision it takes only 8.1 µs ($500\times$ faster). Reveal Audit Discovery 2: contrary to the paper's claim that AFC is computationally lighter than BSC, AFC actually requires twice the instruction count of BSC (18,070 vs 9,275) because of three runtime logarithmic evaluations.
23. **Verdicts on the Paper's Claims.** Read the structured verdict table: method valid, reproduction qualified, small smooth command reproduced, integrate accelerometer reproduced, computation cost claim not supported / debunked, and novel AMC-AFC verified.
24. **Interactive Web Simulation Lab (`web/`).** Showcase the client-side digital twin in `web/quarter_car_afc_lab.html`. Built with zero external dependencies (pure vanilla ES6 and Canvas), it runs a real-time 60 FPS physics engine with four multi-channel oscilloscopes (displacement, valve command, acceleration, dynamic tyre-load ratio with live wheel lift-off warnings). Sliders allow real-time parameter tuning ($k_1, k_2, k_3, k_v$, breathing rates, lag, actuator scale) across bumps, sinusoidal cases, and ISO Class A–D roads, allowing instant live demonstration of barrier dynamics and limit cycles.
25. **Parametric CAD Rig & 3D Kinematics Animation.** Explain the visual kinodynamic validation pipeline: the FreeCAD parametric macro (`cad/quarter_car_rig.FCMacro`) reconstructs the paper's physical apparatus into 12 STEP/STL parts (chassis, hub carrier, tire, hydraulic cylinder, servo valve, sensors). The Blender script (`animation/build_quarter_car_scene.py`) reads simulated displacement CSVs and drives a 3D animated comparison of passive, paper AFC, and AMC-AFC over a bump, visually verifying wheel detachment in paper AFC and continuous contact in AMC-AFC.
26. **Automated Multi-Phase Research Pipeline.** Walk through the fully automated end-to-end research pipeline. The MATLAB suite (`run_phase1.m` through `run_phase4.m`) automates plant calibration, Simulink build, Simscape cross-check, Lyapunov barriers, Monte Carlo sweeps, and AMC-AFC benchmarking. The Python suite (`tools/run_comparative_study.py`) automates numerical co-simulation of all 6 controllers, discrete Jacobian stability evaluation across $k_3$, and vector PDF plotting. The C test harness (`embedded/bench_host.c`) runs 2,000,000-sample timing benchmarks. Every figure and metric in the study is generated reproducibly with single CLI commands.
27. **Conclusions.** Summarize key takeaways: AFC is an elegant formulation that bounds error when the actuator can keep up; calibration reveals the true plant scale; the paper's continuous proof misses sampled-data 88 Hz instability and comfort degradation; novel AMC-AFC repairs these vulnerabilities (skyhook damping injection $\zeta=0.38$, sampled-data stabilization $k_3=60.0 \implies \rho=0.9425$, rattle-space dual barrier preventing wheel lift-off); and the bare-metal C engine delivers deterministic 61.7 ns execution for production automotive ECUs.

## Likely questions

- **Isn't the calibration overfitting?** It fits two factors to four numbers from one experiment. That is why we ran a hold-out test, and the hold-out fails for Case B, which we report transparently.
- **Why is the lumped lag 33 ms when servo valves respond in 3 ms?** It's an effective lag covering the valve, the ESO estimate, and filtering. With 3 ms the fit error is 62–69% (the loop is strongly unstable there, so the exact value depends on the platform). The paper itself mentions actuator delays.
- **Does local instability contradict the paper?** No. Lemma 1 claims boundedness inside the envelope, which still holds; the oscillation is 0.13 mm. It does show that the continuous-time proof doesn't analyse the 50 Hz implementation.
- **Why inject skyhook damping into stage 2 rather than stage 3?** Stage 2 governs the velocity error dynamics $\dot{z}_s - u_1$. Injecting $-k_v \dot{z}_s$ directly augments the physical damping of the sprung mass without disrupting the hydraulic pressure tracking loop.
- **Why set $k_3 = 60.0$ instead of the paper's 216?** At 50 Hz sampling ($T_s = 20\text{ ms}$), the paper's gain $k_3=216$ yields a discrete spectral radius $\rho = 1.2038 > 1.0$, causing an 88 Hz hydraulic limit cycle. Setting $k_3=60.0$ moves the poles strictly inside the unit circle ($\rho = 0.9425 < 1.0$), ensuring discrete asymptotic stability.
- **Is the anti-windup AFC still prescribed-performance control?** It deliberately gives the bound up while the actuator can't deliver, and recovers it afterwards at rate 0.5/s. It's a graceful-degradation design, not a rigid guarantee.
- **Why trust our numbers?** Five independent implementations agree with each other: Simulink to $4.32\times 10^{-16}\text{ m}$, Simscape Multibody to $2\,\mu\text{m}$, the C law to 10 decimal digits. The v1 numbers are reproduced exactly.
- **How do you explain the paper's 4.2 ms execution time on Cortex-M4?** Hardware profiling reveals that bare-metal execution of the AFC algorithm takes only $8.1\,\mu\text{s}$ (with hardware FPU) or $0.18\text{ ms}$ (soft-float). The paper's reported $4.2\text{ ms}$ reflects peripheral ADC/DAC conversions, FreeRTOS context-switching overhead, and interrupt latencies, rather than algorithmic compute time.
- **Why build a Simscape Multibody model when you already had differential equations?** To verify that our 1D lumped-parameter assumptions (vertical translation without parasitic rotational binding, dry friction, or compliance effects) were not artificially masking physical artifacts. Simscape solves 3D joint kinematics independently, and matching it to $2.09\,\mu\text{m}$ proves the mathematical representation is faithful.
- **Can the recursive PPF update accumulate floating-point drift?** No. Because the discrete decay factor satisfies $e^{-\alpha_i T_s} < 1$, any numerical error decays exponentially to zero at every time step toward the fixed asymptote $\mu_{i\infty}$, guaranteeing bounded floating-point stability indefinitely.
